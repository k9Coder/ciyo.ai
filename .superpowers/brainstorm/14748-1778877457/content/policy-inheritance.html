How do policies stack across levels?

A user belongs to a Team, which belongs to a Division, which belongs to the Company. All three levels can have rules. When they conflict — what wins?

Example: Sarah is on the "M&A Team" in the "Legal Division" at "Acme Corp"
Company → Global rule: "Block sending SSNs anywhere"
Division: Legal → "Warn before sending case names to ChatGPT"
Team: M&A → "Block sending anything about Project Zeus"
Sarah → Which rules apply to her?
A

Additive — all rules combine

Sarah gets every rule from every level: company + division + team. No conflicts — rules stack up. Strictest outcome because nothing is ever "overridden away."

Pros

  • Simple mental model
  • No gaps — a rule set at company level always applies

Cons

  • Can't give one team a looser rule than company default
B

Most specific wins — team overrides division overrides company

Team-level rules take precedence. A team can tighten OR loosen a company default. E.g., a trusted internal tools team could be exempt from a company-wide ChatGPT block.

Pros

  • Flexible — teams can have exceptions

Cons

  • A company-level "always block" can be accidentally overridden
  • Harder to reason about what rules are in effect
C

Most restrictive wins — block beats warn, warn beats allow

Rules combine, but when two rules cover the same thing, the stricter action wins. A company "warn" + a team "block" = block. A company "block" can never be loosened by a team.

Pros

  • Security-first: higher authority can never be bypassed
  • Still additive — more rules = more coverage

Cons

  • Slightly more complex to explain to admins
